The Stilyagi Design Language

Agitprop rigour. Jazz-age swagger. University-press authority. A rebellion conducted in grid-ruled margins, under a red pencil, with a saxophone in the next room.

§ 02 · Principles

Seven tenets, and the tension that holds them up

The aesthetic fails as pastiche and succeeds as argument. Agitprop supplies the force; jazz supplies the colour; the Chicago Press supplies the silence between them. No tenet stands alone. Removing any one tips the whole into kitsch.

§

1 · Paper first

Work on a surface, not a screen. Warm cream, graph rules, real margins. The substrate sets the register before a single word lands.

§

2 · Black type, red verdicts

Black carries the argument; red carries the judgement. Use magenta, cyan, and ochre only where music belongs.

§

3 · Two fonts do the work

A condensed display cuts the headline. A classical serif carries the paragraph. A utility sans handles metadata. A monospace speaks for the machine.

§

4 · Evidence before commentary

Show the table, the figure, the span, the byte. Put the claim next to the citation and shut up.

§

5 · Texture, not gradient

Halftone dots, cross-hatch, hazard stripes, rubber-stamp noise. No soft shadow; no plastic glow; no Material elevation.

§

6 · Manicule, not emoji

The pointing hand is the house bullet. It is typographic, not decorative, and it earns its space by pointing at something that matters.

§

7 · Jazz is the accent, not the key

Splatter, sax, music notation, checkerboard — reserved for covers, posters, and the rare celebratory moment. The daily surface is quiet.

!

The tension

Remove the Chicago Press and you get T-shirt design. Remove the agitprop and you get a wedding invitation. Remove the jazz and you get a lint report. Hold all three.

§ 03 · Palette

Four inks, three splashes, one substrate

The palette divides into three registers. Surface sets the stage: warm newsprint cream, lamp-black type, a heavier recess for samizdat. Agitprop carries judgement: poster-red and its darker sibling. Jazz — magenta, cyan, ochre — appears only where music is welcome.

§ 04 · Type

A four-voice system

Display shouts. Editorial argues. Sans labels. Mono testifies. Use each for what it is, and never ask one voice to do another's job.

§ 05 · Grid

A ruled surface, not a frame

Every surface is graph paper. The rule lines at 24 px provide a visible scaffold; all spacing is a multiple of the 8 px unit. The editorial measure caps at 72 ch so paragraphs do not swim. Panels sit inside the grid, not on top of it.

Panel · default

Thin ink border, paper ground, recessed label. The workhorse container for editorial content.

Panel · agitprop

Heavy red border signals judgement. Use for verdicts, diagnostics, failure reports.

Punch-card · standard

Top-right corner cut. Semantic: task in motion. Lifted from Corbusier's vocabulary, retained here for continuity.

Punch-card · blocked

Top-left corner cut and red border. Semantic: blocked, quarantined, obsolete.

AGITPROP

Ink ground, red gutter, paper type. Reserved for posters, verdict blocks, chapter openers.

Panel · recessed

Paper-shade ground for asides, sidebars, and metadata blocks that must read as subordinate.

§ 06 · Motifs

The decoration has a job

Every motif in this system points at something, marks something, or testifies to a printing method. Ornament-for-its-own-sake is not available here.

Manicule — the house bullet

  • Black manicule — the default. Works for any scholarly or structural list.
  • Use when each item is a stand-alone claim earning its own line.
  • Do not use for fewer than two items, or for short sub-items within prose.
  • Red manicule — emphasis within a list, or a verdict list.
  • One colour per list. Mixing manicule colours within a single list is banned.
  • Magenta, cyan, ochre variants exist for cover and poster use only.

Halftone

Halftone at 6 px pitch. Use as ground for posters, diagrams, and icon fills — never behind body text.

Cross-hatch engraving

Parallel-line engraving at 135°. Evokes Chicago-Press plate engraving. Suitable for frames, figure borders, samizdat backgrounds.

Hazard stripe

Corners mark a zone as fragile, deprecated, or pre-release.

Full-width hazard rule, used above warning banners or ship-date markers. One per page maximum.

§ Thesis

Separate the Iron from the Jazz

Do not let every rule parse Markdown. Rules govern regions, not raw bytes. Rust owns file discovery, extraction, structural byte-accounting, and the intermediate representation. Python owns policy, configuration, NLP capabilities, rule execution, and diagnostic rendering.

§ 08 · Restraint

The failure modes of this aesthetic

An aesthetic this loaded breaks in predictable ways. The rules below are observed failure modes, not matters of taste.

Do

One jazz colour per view. Pick magenta, cyan, or ochre, and commit. Two is a carnival; three is a cry for help.

Don't

Never load magenta, cyan and ochre together into the same chart, dashboard, or dense UI.

Do

Stamp a verdict. Obsolete earns its place on a retired spec.

Don't

Never scatter stamps as decoration. A page with three rubber stamps has none.

Do

Reserve splatter for covers. Section openers, posters, launch pages.

Don't

Never put splatter behind body text, tables, code, or forms. The reader's eye cannot work in a paint booth.

Do

Set body in EB Garamond. The serif slows the reader enough to consider the claim.

Don't

Never set body in Big Shoulders. Display type in running text is shouting a paragraph.

Do

Let the grid show. The ruled paper is the system's signature — it reads as scholarly, not decorative.

Don't

Never replace the warm cream substrate with clinical white. The register collapses to generic SaaS on the first stroke.

From Tokens to Clauses

Filed under: grammar nodes

The Stilyagi grammar layer is a deliberately thin translation of a noisy spaCy Doc into a small set of stable, typed nodes on which Python rules may rely without breakage. The design inverts the usual dependency.

Canonical UPos enums are drawn from Universal Dependencies. Four derived syntactic nodes — TokenNode, SentenceNode, NounPhraseNode, and CoordinationNode — are exposed to rule authors. The Embassy Rule admits of no exception: Python rules talk only to stable grammar nodes, never to raw spaCy classes.

A rule that imports spaCy is a rule that will break on the next minor bump. That is not a cost the engine bears.
— Stilyagi grammar brief, § 3

The Embassy Rule simplifies migration, testing, and vendoring. It also makes rule authorship legible to contributors who do not know, and need not know, how the underlying tagger represents morphology.

§ Capability declaration
  • Rules explicitly declare required capabilities: STRUCTURE, POS, DEPENDENCIES.
  • The engine computes the union of requirements and plans the cheapest viable annotation.
  • A purely structural CI run completely bypasses NLP initialization.
!

Breaking change · v0.8 → v0.9

The UPos enum gains three values to cover Universal Dependencies v2.12. Rules that pattern-match on the enum must extend their match arms.
